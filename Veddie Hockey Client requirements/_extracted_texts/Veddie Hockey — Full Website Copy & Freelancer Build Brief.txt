01 / READ THIS FIRST — FREELANCER NOTES

This document contains the complete core marketing-site copy, section order, calls to action, form wording and launch-state instructions. Text labelled H1, H2, Body, Button, Microcopy or FAQ is intended for the website. Production notes and bracketed placeholders are not public copy.

Creative approach: clear offers, tangible player benefits, hockey identity, community and media. This is original Veddie copy; do not imply endorsement or affiliation with Alex Hormozi, Brodie, professional teams or leagues.

Source: Veddie Hockey Playbook, https://docs.google.com/document/d/1HbRdYeMvct8dnAoQbKgFa_42OPXn9sMc7pwhUefwJVM/edit

Latest naming: Champions Cup, Champions League, Tournaments, Leagues and Pickup. Use “Pickup” everywhere, including navigation, forms and page titles. Summer can describe a particular schedule, but is not the product name.

Launch approach: all five product pages appear from day one. Champions Cup and Pickup lead acquisition. Until inventory and delivery are confirmed, use interest forms rather than checkout. Champions League, Tournaments and Leagues accept interest without promising a launch date.

The source targets a spring 2027 Cup and initial pickup in summer 2027, with GTA/southern Ontario as a working market. These are planning assumptions. Public copy below avoids treating them as booked events.

The source proposes a paid founding-team offer. A later marketing idea includes a free inaugural Cup, cash prizes and a captain-tag giveaway. Those economics and terms remain unresolved. Default copy makes no price or prize claim; optional approved-promotion copy appears at the end.

A complete privacy policy, participation terms, waiver, refund rules and competition rules require the operator’s final details. They are launch dependencies, not invented legal text in this document.

Skill levels

D1 — Elite

High-tempo hockey for former pro, college/university and high-level junior players who still play at a strong level.

D2 — Competitive

Strong competitive hockey for suitable junior, AAA and AA backgrounds. Junior experience is reviewed in context, including level, age and time away.

D3 — Intermediate

For experienced organized-hockey and established recreational players who want good pace and team play below D2 intensity.

D4 — Recreational

Lower-tempo hockey for house-league, adult-entry and recreational backgrounds, with players who have comfortable basic game skills.

Microcopy: Playing history guides placement. Current ability determines fit. D4 is not automatically a learn-to-skate program.

Rankings FAQs

Do Champions Cup and Champions League count together?

Yes. Eligible games from both competitions contribute to the same rating within your age and skill category.

Does entering more events automatically improve our ranking?

No. More games provide more evidence, but your rating can rise or fall with results. There are no points simply for entering.

Can a D4 team be compared with a D1 team?

Not through one combined ranking. Each age and skill category has its own table.

Why might a Cup winner not be ranked first?

The Cup title reflects that event’s outcome. The ranking reflects the team’s wider eligible record and opponent strength.

Do regular league standings count?

No. Home-league standings may support Cup qualification, but only verified Champions Cup and Champions League games affect this rating.

Can we challenge a result?

Yes. Contact Veddie with the match reference and the disputed detail. Corrections are reviewed against the official record and reflected in the next published update.

Recommended launch rules — internal specification, proposed for adoption

Use an Elo-style rating rather than cumulative participation points. Start each team at 1,000 in its age/skill category. Both competitions use equal game weighting; do not add title bonuses on top of rated wins. Display trophies and event finishes separately.

For each game: expected result E = 1 / (1 + 10^((opponent rating − team rating)/400)); new rating = old rating + 32 × (S − E). S is 1 for a win, 0.5 for a permitted tie and 0 for a loss. Use both pre-game ratings; retain full precision internally and round only for display. Example: two 1,000-rated teams finish with 1,016 for the winner and 984 for the loser.

Use the official final result, including overtime or shootout, as win/loss. Include pool, knockout and finals games; exclude exhibitions, placement/consolation games, cancelled games, byes and unplayed forfeits. An abandoned match is ineligible unless completed as an official played result under the rules. Apply administrative reversals through an audited correction and chronological recalculation.

Process eligible games in actual chronological order, using scheduled start time then unique match ID for a deterministic tie order. If the Cup later becomes the Champions League finals, count each match once.

Publish numbered ranks after five eligible games; show earlier teams as provisional. Sort by full-precision rating, with exact ties sharing rank. Compare rank movement only within the same category and ranking scope; show “New” for first-time ranked teams.

Maintain an ongoing rating across seasons; provide dated snapshots and archived season-end tables. After 12 months without an eligible match, label the team inactive and remove it from the active table, preserving its history. Review identity and roster continuity before reactivation.

A move to a different age/skill category starts a new 1,000 rating in that category; retain prior-category history separately. Do not let captains reset an existing team by renaming it. Final roster-continuity and appeal rules must be published before launch.

At launch, use regional ranking pools within each age/skill category. Ratings from disconnected pools are not directly comparable. A combined table requires a published transition method and sufficient cross-region play; do not promise a national ranking on day one.

Provide a verified-result entry workflow, unique team and match IDs, competition/category fields, calculation history, corrections and published snapshots. No self-reported result should change a public rating without verification.

MVP: operator-maintained verified ratings and results are acceptable. Do not imply live automation until implemented. Publish a timestamp and show pending results separately.

Use the same dataset for the homepage top-five module, leaderboard and team profiles. Keep the full methodology accessible beside the leaderboard. Test duplicate-game prevention, corrected results, provisional status, exact ties, category moves and inactive teams.
